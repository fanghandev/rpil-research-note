# Literature notes: mixed pinholes / reconstructed vs projection resolution (brain SPECT helmet)
Verification levels: [PDF] = read full text via Undermind; [abs] = abstract/secondary only; [web] = web snippet, unverified.

## Q2/Q3 KEY RESULT: Zannoni, Yang, Meng, "Design Study of an Ultrahigh Resolution Brain SPECT System Using a Synthetic Compound-Eye Camera Design With Micro-Slit and Micro-Ring Apertures", IEEE TMI 40(12):3711-3727, 2021, doi:10.1109/TMI.2021.3096920 [PDF]
HelmetSPECT: ~500 detector modules (502), 20x20 mm detectors, 80x80 pixels of 250 um, minification MF 1:12, focal distance 183.23 mm, sphere radius 196 mm. (Closest published analogue to our system.)
- Uniform: 502 x 1 mm lofthole. Mixed: "168 low-sensitivity-high-resolution loftholes with 500 um diameter, 167 medium-sensitivity-medium-resolution loftholes with 1.5 mm diameter, and 167 high-sensitivity-low-resolution loftholes with 3 mm diameter", interleaved in every ring.
- Quote: "The spreading of the FIM image around the target voxel is narrower in case of micro-slit and micro-ring apertures (16 mm FWHM for micro-slit of 250 um width, 12 mm FWHM for the micro-slit of 150 um width and micro-ring), while clearly wider for loftholes (20 mm FWHM for the 1-mm D lofthole, 26 mm FWHM in the SCE aperture combining 3 types of loft holes of different diameters)." (FIM-image FWHM, not recon FWHM.)
- Mixed-size system: higher sensitivity but LOWEST resolution of all designs; in the S/B 20:1 rod phantom (Fig 8) 4 mm rods resolved by 1 mm lofthole, slits, ring, but NOT by the mixed system. Micro-ring "<4 mm" resolution; others resolve 6 mm rods at S/B 5:1.
- => Directly refutes (in simulation, by a group the PI may know) "mixing in the smallest pinholes recovers resolution": the 0.5 mm third did not rescue the mixed design vs uniform 1 mm. Caveat: the mixed design kept 500 um at only 1/3 and added 3 mm holes (multiplexing/overlap of large-pinhole projections not analysed separately); our own sims independently agree.
- No reconstructed-to-projection resolution factor is stated in the paper.

## Other Meng-group papers
- Meng & Clinthorne, "A modified uniform Cramer-Rao bound for multiple pinhole aperture design", IEEE TMI 23(7):896-902, 2004, doi:10.1109/TMI.2004.828356 [PDF]. Compares 1/3/6/12 x 1 mm pinholes (compact non-redundant arrays) and single-pinhole diameters 0.5-2 mm at target resolutions 1,1.5,2 mm FWHM. Quote: "Due to limitations on the computing power currently available in our lab, the proposed method was applied only for comparing different systems rather than finding the 'optimum' aperture design." No mixed sizes tested; no statement on small+large combination. Findings: more pinholes -> lower variance at same resolution; "A larger pinhole diameter results in lower variance if a worse spatial resolution is acceptable" (optimum pinhole size is monotone in target resolution, i.e. the MUCRB picks a size matched to the target resolution - supports uniform-size-per-target-resolution). Recon centre resolution 1.52 mm FWHM identical across designs (LIR-based). Resolution here = achieved reconstructed LIR FWHM, not projection resolution.
- Meng & Li, "A Vector Uniform Cramer-Rao Bound for SPECT System Design", IEEE TNS 56(1):81-90, 2009, doi:10.1109/TNS.2008.2006609 [PDF]. 200 um pinholes, 16/25/36/49-pinhole apertures; "even without overlapping between projections through individual pinholes, the best imaging performance ... was achieved with the 16-pinhole aperture." Detector pixel 1 -> 0.5 mm "led to a significant improvement in ROI quantitation. Further improved resolution offered only a modest performance gain." No mixed apertures; no projection-to-recon rule.
- Meng & Li, "SPECT System Optimization Against A Discrete Parameter Space", PMB 58:3037, 2013, doi:10.1088/0031-9155/58/9/3037 [abs]: optimal imaging time distribution over "virtual detectors" (could in principle weight different aperture sizes by time - a relevant framework for non-uniform weighting but not tested as mixed pinholes in abstract).
- Lai & Meng, MRC-SPECT-II (inverted compound-eye), PMB 63:045008, 2018, doi:10.1088/1361-6560/aaa4fb [PDF]: 1536 micro-cameras, 8x8 pinholes of 300 um (not mixed), 0.1 mm pixels, demagnifying (~0.45 from listed distances); reconstructed resolution 0.45-0.55 mm (resolution phantom); "The ultra-high 3D resolution of the detector enabled MRC-SPECT-II to use a demagnification design without sacrificing resolution". No claimed factor vs projection resolution; no sub-pixel claim.
- Mandot et al, NSS/MIC 2025 (doi:10.1109/nss/mic/rtsd57106.2025.11286925) [abs]: dual-FOV brain SPECT, 320 CZT detectors, 2 mm pinholes for 20 cm whole-brain mode, micro-ring 0.25 mm for 5 cm mode; simulation "resolution of 6 mm for the larger FOV mode and 1 mm for the high-resolution mode". Relies on hardware-implemented sub-pixel/angular shifts (super-resolution sampling) - i.e. their route to better resolution is non-redundant sampling and motion, not mixed sizes. Also Mandot 2024 "NeuroScope" doi:10.1109/nss/mic/rtsd57108.2024.10656162 [abs only].
- Others (titles only, not read): Meng et al, "Adaptive apertures for microscopic SPECT imaging" (2007); Fu/Tan/Meng NSS 2007 "Aperture design for ultra-high resolution SPECT systems..." doi:10.1109/NSSMIC.2007.4436718; Meng et al NIM A 2009 doi:10.1016/J.NIMA.2008.11.149 (I-125 mouse brain SPEM, I-EMCCD).

## Q1: origin of "reconstructed is 2-2.5x better than analytical" - NOT FOUND as a stated rule
No source found that states a general factor 2-2.5. Evidence collected:
- U-SPECT-class papers: reconstructed resolution is "significantly better than system resolution" but numbers are ~1x-1.3x, not 2x: 0.6 mm pinholes -> 0.45 mm reconstructed; 0.3 mm pinholes -> <0.35 mm [web snippet, Vastenhouw/Beekman "System calibration and statistical image reconstruction for ultra-high resolution stationary pinhole SPECT", IEEE TMI 2007 - unverified, snippet only]. These are magnifying geometries.
- MRC-SPECT-II: 300 um pinholes -> 0.45 mm reconstructed (ratio ~1.5 vs pinhole diameter; not a system-resolution comparison).
- Pinhole resolution recovery (multi-ray/PSF modelling): improves resolution/noise tradeoff, no factor stated in abstracts (Van Audenhaege/Vanhove 2007 EJNMMI; Soh/Seret 2006; Seret 2007) [abs/title].
- Plausible sources of PI's rule of thumb (my inference, unverified): (i) conflating FWHM of a point-source projection (blur kernel) with achieved recon resolution under deconvolution-like OSEM for high-contrast, high-count objects; (ii) resolution-recovery literature in parallel-hole SPECT (Flash3D/Evolution) where recovery gives ~1.3-2x improvement in FWHM at depth but is limited by the Gaussian-model and iteration count; (iii) "pinhole diameter/2"-type rules for sampling. None confirmed. Applies only when system response is well modelled, objects high-contrast, and many iterations; for minifying geometries dominated by detector pixel term, system matrix modelling can recover information only up to the pixel-sampling (aliasing) limit, so a factor ~2 on the pixel term requires sub-pixel sampling diversity (which is exactly Mandot/Meng's "hardware super-resolution sampling" idea).
- Johnson/Moore/Metzler PMB 2016: only projection resolution; no recon relation.

## Q4 Pinhole resolution-effective diameter
- Accorsi & Metzler, "Analytic determination of the resolution-equivalent effective diameter of a pinhole collimator", IEEE TMI 23(6):750-763, 2004, doi:10.1109/TMI.2004.826951 [abstract/secondary only]. Key message (secondary): the commonly used d_e (Paix/Anger) "was originally derived to account for penetration in sensitivity calculations"; for normal incidence Paix's expression tends to overestimate the resolution-equivalent diameter for FWHM, Anger's is a better approximation; at grazing incidence both overestimate significantly. 
- Accorsi & Metzler, "Resolution-effective diameters for asymmetric-knife-edge pinhole collimators", IEEE TMI 24(12):1637-1646, 2005, doi:10.1109/TMI.2005.857229 [title only]. Metzler & Accorsi, "Resolution- versus sensitivity-effective diameter in pinhole collimation: experimental verification", PMB 50:5005, 2005 [title only].
- Metzler, Bowsher, Smith, Jaszczak, "Analytic determination of pinhole collimator sensitivity with penetration", IEEE TMI 20(8):730-741, 2001 (sensitivity-effective).
- Formulas:
  * Sensitivity-effective (Paix 1967 / Anger): d_e,sens = sqrt( d^2 + 2 d tan(alpha/2)/mu + 2 tan^2(alpha/2)/mu^2 ) (Paix, as commonly quoted - from memory, not verified here); the simpler Anger form d_e = sqrt(d (d + (2/mu) tan(alpha/2))) is the one used by Ghosh et al 2024 [PDF, Gho24b, Research Square rs-4406814, doi unknown]: R_coll = d_e (a+b)/a, R_intr = pixel/M, R_sys = sqrt(R_intr^2 + R_coll^2), M = a/b (a = pinhole-detector, b = source-pinhole).
  * Resolution-effective for FWHM (first-order, normal incidence; this is what U-SPECT papers cite, web snippet): d_eff,R = d + (ln 2 / mu) tan(alpha/2). Quoted in a U-SPECT design paper as "d_eff,R = d + ln(2)/mu tan(alpha/2)". Patent-quoted off-axis forms (garbled, verify against the paper): d_re,par = d + (ln2/mu)(tan(a/2) - cot(theta/2))... and d_re,perp = sqrt((d + (ln2/mu) tan(a/2) sin theta)^2 - (ln2/mu)^2 cos^2 theta). Direction dependent: different in the two directions at oblique incidence; both treated incidence angle theta from the aperture plane.
  * => Use d_re = d + (ln2/mu) tan(alpha/2) for FWHM (linear in d, not quadrature); this is smaller than Paix/Anger sensitivity width, so our current Paix-based number is a (conservative) upper bound as assumed. Check exact expression in the 2004 TMI paper before publishing.
- Goulet & Joy, Med Phys 7:571, 1980: measured LSF FWHM ~20% better than Anger-index prediction (another "analytical is pessimistic" data point for projection FWHM, only ~1.2x).

## Zannoni 2021 setup
- Apertures: uniform 502 x 1 mm lofthole (same open area as 150 um slit); mixed 168 x 0.5 + 167 x 1.5 + 167 x 3 mm; micro-slits 150 um x 6 mm, 250 um x 5 mm (each slit rotated by angle theta); micro-ring (annulus width 250 um). Slit/ring widths set equal to detector intrinsic resolution. Detector pixels 250 um, 1 mm DOI. MF 1:12 (SS II-B, pp.4-7).
- Central sensitivity (SS III-C pp.14-15, Fig 6): 1 mm lofthole 0.11%; 150 um slit 0.19%; micro-ring 1.38% (>1% over central 10 cm). Mixed/250um slit values only in Fig 6 (not given in text); text range 0.11-1.38%.
- Central FIM FWHM (Fig 5, p.13): 1 mm 20 mm; mixed 26 mm; 250 um slit 16; 150 um slit 12; ring 12. Ring FIM peak 2.5x the mixed design.
- Reconstruction (SS II-D/E, pp.8-11): 3D OSEM, 8 subsets, no post-filter, 96^3 voxels of 2 mm; hot-rod phantom 1 mCi Tc-99m rods, bkg 0.5 mCi (S/B 20:1) or 2 mCi (5:1), 30 min; brain phantoms 2e9 photons; iterations selected per design (Fig 8 captions 247/381/255/173/385; Fig 9: 126/241/166/122/276, order 1mm, mixed, 250 slit, 150 slit, ring); ictal phantom noiseless 500 it; lesion phantom 50 it.
- Rods resolved: S/B 20:1 -> 4 mm rods resolved by 1 mm lofthole, both slits, ring; mixed resolves only down to 6 mm. S/B 5:1 -> ring resolves 4 mm; all others (incl. 1 mm and mixed) down to 6 mm. Defrise (Fig 7): mixed has degraded central disk resolution. Ictal perfusion (Fig 10): mixed performs worst overall; lesion phantom (Fig 11): slit/ring better for 5 mm lesion.

## Verbatim: rationale for mixed sizes (SS II-B.3, p.6)
"The second set of lofthole apertures consists of three different groups of lofthole apertures of different diameters: 168 low-sensitivity-high-resolution loftholes with 500 um diameter, 167 medium-sensitivity-medium-resolution loftholes with 1.5 mm diameter (Fig.2E and F), and 167 high-sensitivity-low-resolution loftholes with 3 mm diameter."
"The specific combination of the three lofthole diameters is chosen to allow the system to sample the object at high spatial frequencies, while maintaining a relatively high sensitivity."

## Verbatim: why mixed underperformed
- SS III-D.1, p.15: "For the collimator with 3 types of loftholes of different diameters, the angular sampling provided by each type of lofthole is sparser than the case of 1-mm D lofthole collimator. Additionally, only the two smallest loftholes could allow to resolve the disk features at the center of the FOV. The reduction of the number of loftholes able to visualize the feature and the sparser angular sampling led to the degraded resolution in the central area of the FOV, so that the disk features are not well-resolved."
- SS IV, p.18: "The combination of 3 types of loftholes having different diameters improves the system sensitivity but offers the lowest spatial resolution in this study."
- Overlap/multiplexing, SS III-A p.12: "To improve the tradeoffs between effective sensitivity and imaging resolution, one needs to minimize the projection overlapping to reduce the ambiguity on the incident direction of the detected photons." SS IV p.18: "...the SCE geometry does not introduce projection overlapping. This prevents the multiplexing of projections, which is known to degrade the image quality for a modest gain in detection efficiency."
- Detector/minification, SS II-C p.7: "...the large minification geometry makes the DOI capability indispensable to achieve a high spatial resolution needed in brain imaging applications." Slit/ring widths "set equal to the intrinsic resolution of the simulated detector" (II-B.1 p.4, II-B.2 p.6).
- (e) No verbatim statement relating reconstructed resolution to projection/geometric/intrinsic resolution; no formula decomposition in this paper.
- Interpretation (mine): their explanation is angular-sampling dilution - only the 1/3 small-hole modules carry high-frequency information; large holes (3 mm, penetration SNR 420) contribute smooth blur. Equivalent to our finding that uniform beats mix at equal total time.

## IMPORTANT: probable source of PI's hypothesis - DB-SPECT (not Meng group)
Feng, Hashemi, Soleymani, Ottensmeyer, Sabet (MGH/Harvard), "DB-SPECT, a Fixed-Gantry SPECT Scanner for Dynamic Brain Imaging: Design Concept and First Results", IEEE NSS/MIC 2022, doi:10.1109/NSS/MIC44845.2022.10399152 [abstract only, no PDF in Undermind]. 118 modules, 5x5x1 cm CsI:Tl + SiPM, 210 mm FOV, lofthole collimators. Quote: "at the same system sensitivity of the conventional dual head gamma camera (0.01%), calculated resolution is 4.05 mm FWHM while some of the 2 mm dia. and all of the 2.65 mm dia. hot rods in a Derenzo phantom are resolved. We further implemented a mixed collimator design where the 118 collimators are divided in to 4 groups with each group having different collimator dimension ranging from 0.5 to 2.0 mm. This resulted in high sensitivity thanks to the collimator groups with larger hole sizes and high resolution where the groups with small collimator hole contributed to recovered resolution. This mixed collimator design features 5.6x sensitivity gain compared to the dual head gamma camera while 2.65 mm dia. hot rods are partially resolved." => Four groups, 0.5-2.0 mm, CsI, ~118 modules: very close to our design and almost certainly where "resolution recovered by the smallest pinhole" and "calculated resolution 4.05 mm vs Derenzo resolves 2-2.65 mm" (ratio ~1.5-2) come from. Note: their comparison is mixed (5.6x sens) vs a dual-head camera at 0.01%, NOT vs a uniform aperture at equal sensitivity; the abstract gives no uniform-vs-mixed comparison. Worth following up (full paper / later journal version by Sabet group; not searched).

# Sabet group mixed collimator

Only source: Feng et al NSS/MIC 2022 abstract (3 pp. proceedings; IEEE Xplore page returned empty to fetch; no PDF in Undermind). So the following cannot be answered from public text: mixed vs uniform at equal sensitivity/time; counts; recon settings; rods per design; whether a uniform design at 5.6x sensitivity was simulated. The abstract compares mixed only to a dual-head camera (and to the uniform design at 0.01%): [abs only] doi:10.1109/NSS/MIC44845.2022.10399152.
Abstract facts (verbatim, [abs]): "at the same system sensitivity of the conventional dual head gamma camera (0.01%), calculated resolution is 4.05 mm FWHM while some of the 2 mm dia. and all of the 2.65 mm dia. hot rods in a Derenzo phantom are resolved." ... "mixed collimator design ... 4 groups ... 0.5 to 2.0 mm. ... groups with small collimator hole contributed to recovered resolution. ## What "calculated resolution" means in this group (from sister system DC-SPECT, same lab, same authors; [PDF full text read])
Feng, Worstell, Kupinski, Furenlid, Sabet, "Resolution recovery on list mode MLEM reconstruction for Dynamic Cardiac SPECT system", arXiv:2212.14505 (Dec 2022; local copy text extracted). Verbatim:
"The system resolution in the centre of the FOV can be calculated theoretically by the following factors, as indicated in [19]: R_system = sqrt(R_coll^2 + R_int^2), R_coll = d_eff (l+b)/l, d_eff = sqrt(d * (d + 2 mu^-1 tan(alpha/2)))" where l = collimator length (pinhole-detector), b = pinhole centre to FOV centre, d = pinhole side, alpha acceptance angle, mu = tungsten attenuation. "Given collimator length 87 mm, distance from the centre of the pinhole to the centre of the FOV 253 mm, pinhole size 2.25 mm and acceptance angle ~42 deg, the theoretical system resolution is estimated as ~ 10 mm." Sensitivity g = d_eff^2/(16 b^2).
=> "Calculated resolution" = geometric formula at FOV centre with Anger/Paix-type (sensitivity) d_eff, i.e. the SAME (pessimistic) quadrature formula we use; geometry is minifying (l/b = 0.34, similar to our 27 mm / 60-240 mm), detector 2x2 mm converging pixels (CsI:Tl, 10 mm thick, laser-processed). The 4.05 mm DB-SPECT figure is very likely computed by this formula (inference; DB-SPECT paper not seen), at FOV centre (unverified).
Reconstructed vs calculated (DC-SPECT): abstract: "10 mm FWHM geometrical resolution (without resolution recovery) and 0.07% sensitivity"; after list-mode MLEM with image-based spatially-variant PSF deconvolution: "4.5 mm FWHM central spatial resolution can be achieved ... at 0.07% system sensitivity even with relatively low counts (2x10^5)". Ratio 10/4.5 = 2.2x (my calculation; paper states no explicit factor). This is almost certainly the origin of the PI's "reconstructed 2-2.5x better than analytical".
Caveats on the 4.5 mm number: (i) "resolution" there is read from a modified Derenzo phantom with rod diameters 7/6/5.5/4.5 mm (1 mm voxels, post-Gaussian smoothing sigma=1.2 on the central slice, 400 iterations, 2e5 or 2e6 counts, 154 MBq 20 s, 2.1e6 events); i.e. smallest rod resolved/profile, noise-free ground truth known -> not a FWHM of an LIR; (ii) only first row of 20 of 80 heads reconstructed; (iii) separate simulated point-source FWHM was used only for PSF fitting (3D Gaussian fitted to point source reconstructed 40 iterations; MSE 1.28e-7); (iv) no scatter/attenuation; (v) the PSF deconvolution is an additional image-space step: MLEM CNR/CRC 4.2/8.7 -> invariant PSF 4.5/10.1 -> SV-PSF 5.5/12.2 (2e5 counts, 4.5 mm rods); (vi) MC system matrix gave no advantage there. Same group's other numbers: DC-SPECT 2020 abstract (Blackberg, Sajedi...Sabet, NSS/MIC 2020): "Note that the resolution values are without employing resolution recovery techniques" (4.1 mm at 0.01% sensitivity, 15x gamma camera at 15 mm). JNM 2025 supplement abstract (Feng et al, "Spatial Resolution Evaluation of DC-SPECT System: Simulation Study", jnm.snmjournals.org/content/66/supplement_1/251861 [abs summary only, fetched text paraphrased]): point sources at 1.5 mm spacing, list-mode MLEM, resolution 1.78-2.28 mm without scatter, 3.28-5.96 mm with scatter; Derenzo MC system matrix 5.5 mm vs analytical solid-angle matrix 8 mm.
So: the Sabet group's own convention = "calculated (geometric, Anger d_eff, FOV centre)" vs "reconstructed with resolution modelling"; factors reported ~1.5-2.2 depending on phantom/post-filter; no formal rule stated.

## Implication for our design discussion (my inference)
- A 4.05 mm "calculated" resolution for the uniform DB-SPECT at 0.01% sensitivity along with 2-2.65 mm rods resolved means they too see reconstructed rod resolution ~1.5-2x better than the formula, in a minifying CsI/2 mm-pixel geometry - consistent with our own OSEM 5-7 mm vs 11-15 mm count-weighted.
- Their mixed design at 5.6x sensitivity resolved less (2.65 mm only partially) than the uniform small-aperture design, which is the standard sensitivity-resolution trade, not evidence that mixing beats uniform at equal sensitivity. Their abstract has no such comparison; Zannoni 2021 does and finds the opposite.
